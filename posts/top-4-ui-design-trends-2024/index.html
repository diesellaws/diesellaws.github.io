Top 4 UI Design Trends for 2024

October 11, 2024

As we approach the end of 2024, UI design continues to be shaped by new technologies and shifting user demands. Designers are focusing not just on aesthetics but on immersive, personalised and responsible digital experiences.

1. AI is now across every part of UI design

AI has become a powerful tool for designers, changing how concepts are explored and refined. It offers near-instant variety across wording, layouts and interactive components, allowing teams to prototype and iterate at remarkable speed. Its reliance on vast datasets, copyright questions and limited source transparency remain important concerns.

AI in UI

  • Concepting and ideation: Generate wording, layout structures and visual directions quickly.
  • Wireframing: Create and refine structural frameworks earlier in the process.
  • High-fidelity variations: Explore colour, typography and component options at scale.
  • Prototyping and code integration: Turn design elements into functional prototypes for testing.

2. 3D elements and immersive visuals

3D elements have moved beyond niche uses into mainstream interface design. Shapes, models and rich visuals add depth and a tactile quality that can make a product more memorable.

Trends in 3D UI design

  • Textured shapes that add visual layers and depth.
  • Subtle animated effects that support interaction without distracting from it.
  • Immersive environments for commerce and entertainment products.

3. Neo-brutalism meets glassmorphism

Designers are combining the stark contrast, raw shapes and unpolished character of neo-brutalism with smooth translucent surfaces. The result feels bold while retaining hierarchy and depth.

4. Sustainable and inclusive design practices

Energy efficiency and accessibility are now core design considerations. Dark and high-contrast modes, adaptive navigation, optimised assets and screen-reader testing make interfaces more useful to more people.

  • Optimise image and video assets to reduce unnecessary resource use.
  • Offer adaptable display modes for different visual needs.
  • Test with assistive technologies throughout the design process.
